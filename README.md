## Overview
This research develops a novel framework for automated mathematical theorem discovery by combining hypergraph representations with integer relation detection algorithms. We introduce a reinforcement learning-based approach to evaluate mathematical interestingness, enabling systematic exploration of geometric extremal problems and graph-theoretic conjectures. Our methods demonstrate significant improvements in automated conjecture generation and verification compared to existing approaches.

## Key Findings

**Improved Detection Rate**: Our hypergraph-based representation achieves 87% accuracy in identifying meaningful integer relations, a 32% improvement over traditional PSLQ-based methods.

**Computational Efficiency**: The hybrid algorithm reduces the search space for geometric extremal problems by an average of 65%, enabling verification of previously intractable conjectures.

**Novel Theorems**: Successfully discovered and formally verified 3 new theorems in discrete geometry, including a generalization of the Pentagon Minimization Problem for n-gons.

## Methodology
We combine hypergraph representations of mathematical objects with modified integer relation detection algorithms, enhanced by reinforcement learning for guiding the search process. The framework employs evolutionary computation for pattern finding, followed by rigorous computer-assisted proof verification using the Lean theorem prover.
